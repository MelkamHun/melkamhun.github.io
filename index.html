Melkamu Hunegnaw Asmare

Advancing AI and technology for
affordable, accessible healthcare.

I am a postdoctoral researcher and research coordinator at the Leuven Centre for Affordable Health Technology and eMedia Research Lab, KU Leuven. My work connects health AI, biomedical signal processing and digital health with the practical needs of healthcare systems.

I am a versatile, interdisciplinary researcher with a proven track record in securing research funding, building international partnerships and co-leading funded projects. My work spans biomedical engineering, health AI, digital care, responsible innovation and inclusive education, with a focus on technologies that are clinically useful, affordable and practical in settings with limited resources.

I bring extensive experience collaborating with hospitals, industry, public institutions and universities across Africa, Asia and Europe. My collaborations include UZ Leuven and Tikur Anbessa Specialized Hospital in Ethiopia; Ugani Prosthetics, Philips, Axiles Bionics and VarmacoAddis; the Ethiopian Public Health Institute (EPHI); the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA); and multiple university partners. These collaborations connect academic research with clinical needs, technology development and public health priorities.

I received my PhD in Engineering Technology from KU Leuven in 2022, specializing in Health Innovation (Biomedical Engineering) at the Center for Dynamical Systems, Signal Processing, and Data Analytics (STADIUS). Supervised by Prof. Bart Vanrumste, my doctoral research focused on rheumatic heart disease detection. My academic and professional experience spans Africa, Asia and Europe.

Research interests

01

AI for screening & diagnosis

Machine learning for ECG, heart sounds and medical imaging, with applications in rheumatic heart disease and glaucoma screening. A focus on robust evaluation and accessible diagnostic tools.

02

Affordable & sustainable health technology

Healthcare technologies designed around affordability, usability, maintenance and real clinical workflows. Human-centred design and implementation in resource-constrained settings.

03

Equitable digital health & responsible AI

Explainable AI, health data governance and inclusive digital care. International partnerships that connect technical research with local needs, education and health-system capacity.

A funded thematic programme to develop and evaluate telehealth for communities affected by conflict and disaster in Ethiopia. AAU Thematic Research Fund · 2025–2026 callAcoustic & Motion Sensor–Based AI for Early Knee Osteoarthritis DiagnosisConnecting engineering, clinical practice and health policy

A three-year research programme in Ethiopia combining knee-joint acoustics and wearable motion sensing for early osteoarthritis diagnosis. Its three components address sensor design and AI/data fusion, clinical evaluation and implementation, and health-system and policy sustainability. Partners span Addis Ababa University, the University of Gondar and KU Leuven.

My role: Programme coordinator; co-lead, technological innovation and data science.

Development and evaluation of low-cost phono-arthrography sensors and machine learning for knee osteoarthritis screening and severity assessment. The seed project combines joint sounds, clinical information and movement analysis, with clinical collaboration at Black Lion Hospital. My role: Co-lead.

Grand Challenges Ethiopia · Seed grantAI-Assisted Volume Sweep Imaging for Prenatal UltrasoundExpanding ultrasound access in Ethiopia

A project combining standardised ultrasound sweeps with offline-capable AI to support prenatal screening by trained midwives and other non-specialists. The work at St. Paul’s Hospital Millennium Medical College focuses on fetal presentation, placental location and basic biometry, with clinician review and a planned 15-month pilot.
